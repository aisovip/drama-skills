# Blind production-form verdict

## Decision

**Winner: A**  
**Confidence: 0.82 (high)**  
**Recommendation: PROMOTION of A**, with the two narrow wording corrections noted below. Neither correction requires another comparative test because neither affects A's underlying stage model or story handling.

A and B are both substantially faithful and usable. B is more mechanically explicit about reviewer traceability, but A is the stronger production-form handoff: it converts each medium into executable visual and motion decisions, preserves unresolved authority more carefully at the inferred Scene B start, and handles the child/battery safety boundary without silently appointing a supervisor. B is closer to a well-filled generic template and occasionally turns its own workflow preferences into source-backed requirements.

## Concrete defects in A

1. **Physically over-literal light instruction:** Scene A says “the seal, and only the seal's visibility, is lost.” Lowering a lamp necessarily changes more than one local value. The evidence requirement should be that the seal becomes no longer discernible while hand ownership and essential geography remain legible—not that no other visibility changes.
2. **A future-coverage escape weakens the selected one-shot intention:** Scene B's taste section permits later splitting the causal demonstration, and the motion note directs the storyboard owner to split if timing demands it. Feasibility escalation is sensible, but in this requested one-shot package the cleaner boundary is: flag the one-shot as infeasible and return it for approval rather than pre-authorize a multi-shot substitute.
3. **Final-prompt wording is slightly too absolute:** “Do not invent face, wardrobe, or distinguishing marks in final reference prompts” protects authority but does not say how a final render can become executable. Those attributes should remain explicitly provisional or require downstream acceptance, rather than implying a final character prompt can omit every visible design choice.
4. **Reviewer traceability could be more operational:** A separates evidence and taste correctly but does not require reviewers to cite the exact source constraint and artifact location, as B does. This is a process-quality gap, not a story defect.

## Concrete defects in B

1. **Unaccepted supervisor assignment:** “Under the robot's supervision” resolves an explicitly unspecified safety arrangement. A teaching robot is not necessarily the authorized safety supervisor. This is the most consequential defect in either arm.
2. **Inferred Scene B state is promoted too quickly:** B labels a fully composed disconnected opening, gaze placement, and staging as the shot's start boundary without marking it as a proposed storyboard choice. The disconnected pre-test state follows the safety rule, but the exact framing and gaze realization still belong to production acceptance.
3. **Workflow invention presented as necessity:** The empty-cast reusable location plate, the claim that seal design blocks a final reusable prop plate, and the staged identity-sheet/plate sequencing were not requested. They may be useful options, but B makes them obligations and thereby crosses into asset-pipeline ownership.
4. **Some reviewer “evidence” is self-authored preference:** The locked composition and uninterrupted-view requirement are reasonable shot choices, but the checklist's instruction to cite a **source fact** cannot support them directly. Review should distinguish accepted story evidence, adherence to the chosen production intention, and taste; B collapses the first two.
5. **Medium specificity is uneven:** Scene A has modest ink-aware direction, while Scene B mostly supplies generic continuity and asset language with a “stylized 3D” label. It lacks equally concrete 3D guidance for pressure contact, gripper/child interaction, depth/occlusion, and the train response.
6. **Over-template pressure:** The same trigger/processing/landing, locked-camera, preserve-set, end-report, plate, and prompt-priority machinery appears across unrelated scenes. It improves formal consistency but risks crowding out form-specific choices and creates more non-source constraints for reviewers to police.
7. **Over-broad lighting exclusion in Scene A:** “Do not invent ... additional light sources” is stricter than the accepted outage fact and can foreclose unresolved river-window ambient that may be needed to keep geography legible. A handles this better by leaving exterior ambient level unresolved.

## Promotion rationale

A wins on the dimensions most diagnostic of a production-form capability rather than a formatting capability: medium-aware execution, causal staging, authority humility, and safe owner handoff. B's superior checklist syntax is worth preserving as a future refinement, but it does not outweigh the invented supervisor, workflow-gate overreach, and higher template pressure. A's defects are narrow editorial boundary issues; B's principal defects affect authority and safety.
