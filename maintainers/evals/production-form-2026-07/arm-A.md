# Production-form handoff

## Scene A — vertical ink-brush dynamic comic

### Director-facing production note

The dramatic unit is **a visible act of concealment without an explanation**. Let the audience first register that the seal is in the lamp's light, then see the clerk deliberately remove that visibility by lowering the wick. Do not add readable notice copy, explanatory imagery, or a reaction that explains the seal's meaning. Preserve the pawnshop geography in the same image logic: counter between the pair, shelves behind the clerk, river window behind the collector.

For the ink-brush form, keep people, hands, lamp, folded notice, and seal as hard recognition silhouettes. Use softer wash/negative space for depth: shelves as a denser rear layer behind her and the river window as a quieter rear layer behind him. The limited-motion budget belongs to the clerk's right hand, wick/flame, her controlled gaze, and the shrinking light pool; bodies, props, counter, shelves, and window otherwise hold. Ink spread or brush flutter must not obscure hand ownership, the seal, or geography.

### Essential asset and image-prompt decisions

- **Pawnshop / outage view:** one stable location with the accepted counter, shelf, and river-window relationships. Powered fixtures remain off. The exact exterior ambient level is unresolved.
- **Clerk and collector:** their identity/Look anchors are not supplied; do not invent face, wardrobe, or distinguishing marks in final reference prompts. Their blocking sides are scene state, not identity.
- **Oil lamp:** managed prop with a holdable left-hand position and an operable wick control. Start state: lit strongly enough for the seal to be visible. End state: wick lowered until the seal is no longer visible; lamp remains in her left hand. Exact lamp construction and whether the flame remains barely lit are unresolved.
- **Folded notice:** managed prop, folded throughout, in the collector's right hand throughout. Treat the seal as `graphic_only`; treat the notice copy as `no_readable_text`. The seal needs a stable accepted emblem design before a final prop plate, but no letters, numbers, or prose may be improvised.
- **Prompt projection:** a vertical, single-instant composition must prioritize the lamp-lit seal and the clerk's access to the wick while retaining both geographic rear anchors. Repeat only the crucial hand/prop and visibility facts; do not turn temporary lighting, gaze, or pose into character identity.

### One shot and frozen keyframe intention

**Shot:** A restrained two-person view from the end of the counter, with the counter maintaining separation and the lamp pool organizing attention. The collector's right-hand notice enters the same readable visual zone as the clerk's lamp. The shot exists to turn “the seal can be seen” into “she has chosen that it cannot be seen,” not to disclose why. Keep the camera fixed so the light change, not decorative reframing, creates the reveal.

**Authoritative start:** clerk on the shelf side, oil lamp in her left hand; collector on the river-window side, folded notice in his right hand; counter between them; seal visible in the lamp pool.  
**Authoritative end:** the clerk has lowered the wick so the seal is out of sight; lamp remains in her left hand and notice remains in his right; neither prop transfers or unfolds; geography is unchanged.

**Frozen start keyframe:** one still instant before the wick adjustment. The vertical frame keeps both rear anchors legible, the counter as the separator, the folded notice's seal inside the lamp pool, and the clerk's gaze briefly fixed on that seal while the rest of her performance remains contained. Her right hand is free and near—but not yet operating—the wick control. No ordered action, dimming process, or end-state darkness appears in this frame.

### Motion-spec intention

Start exactly from the frozen boundary. The clerk receives the seal visually, suppresses any overt acknowledgment, then uses her **right hand** to lower the wick while her **left hand continues holding the lamp**. The light pool contracts; the seal, and only the seal's visibility, is lost. The collector keeps the folded notice in his right hand. Hold the camera and all set layers; only the necessary hand action, flame/light response, and restrained gaze change move. End-report fields must match the authoritative end for position, gaze, both hands, held props, fold state, seal visibility, and geography. Duration, sound, and the precise amount of reaction hold are unresolved; derive them from the action rather than speeding it to fit an invented duration.

### Reviewer checklist

**Evidence gates — revise if violated**

- Start and end preserve the specified hands and props; the clerk's right-hand wick action does not transfer the lamp from her left.
- The notice stays folded and in the collector's right hand; no readable copy is introduced.
- The seal is visible at start and out of sight only as the result of the wick reduction.
- Counter, shelves, and river window keep their accepted relationships.
- The keyframe is a single start instant and does not contain dimming or the terminal state.
- Motion gives a causal, ordered path and reports the accepted end rather than rewriting it.
- Ink effects do not erase the evidence needed to perceive her choice.

**Taste choices — do not block without an accepted override**

- Exact ink density, dry/wet edge balance, camera height, framing tightness, reaction restraint, and length of the final hold.
- Whether the lowered flame remains visible, provided the seal is not and the accepted hand/prop states remain intact.

**Unresolved:** character/Look designs; seal emblem design; lamp mechanism and terminal flame level; exterior ambient light; duration and sound plan.

## Scene B — stylized 3D educational short

### Director-facing production note

Make the diagnosis change through one observable comparison, not through labels: pressure at the loose contact makes the train move; releasing the same contact makes it stop. The child's attention must shift from the presumed motor fault to the tested contact because of that result. Preserve the robot's identity silhouette—square head, single blue antenna on its left, orange insulated grippers—and give contact, release, and train response convincing 3D contact weight. Avoid idle bouncing or unrelated background motion; one causal unit owns the shot.

The battery pack is disconnected before and after the supervised test and connected only for that test. The supervision arrangement and the safe physical method by which the child participates in the joint press are not specified; production must resolve them before final choreography and must not improvise exposed-contact handling.

### Essential asset and image-prompt decisions

- **Robot identity:** stable square head, one blue antenna on the robot's left, orange insulated grippers. Body proportions, remaining colors/materials, and any other features are unresolved and must not compete with those anchors.
- **Child:** identity and Look are unresolved; do not infer age beyond “child,” wardrobe, or appearance.
- **Diagnostic prop system:** track the toy train, motor, battery pack, loose contact, and connection point as one functional continuity system. Required state chain: `disconnected / train stopped` → supervised-test connection → `contact pressed / train moving` → `contact released / train stopped` → disconnected after the observation. Exact train design and contact geometry are unresolved.
- **Text:** generate no explanatory labels as scene facts. If editorial explanation is later required, reserve a clean postproduction-safe area; exact wording, placement, and even whether text is needed remain unresolved.
- **Prompt projection:** the start image must make the separated battery connection, stationary train, loose-contact area, robot anchors, and the child's mistaken attention toward the motor simultaneously legible. Do not use a full asset-bible rewrite or add screens, diagrams, warnings, or copy not supplied by the scene.

### One shot and frozen keyframe intention

**Shot:** A continuous causal demonstration in a stable three-quarter view that keeps the loose contact, the train, the robot's grippers, and the child's receiving reaction readable without cutting away. The camera does not chase the train; the movement entering and leaving the train is the evidence. The shot begins with the child's attention on the motor and ends with attention on the contact/result relationship.

**Proposed start boundary for storyboard acceptance:** battery pack visibly disconnected; train stationary; contact unpressed; child looking toward the motor; robot adjacent to the loose-contact area with the square head, left blue antenna, and orange grippers unobscured.  
**Accepted-result end projected as a shot boundary:** after the joint press has made the train move and release has made it stop, neither participant is pressing; the child has visibly registered the comparison; the battery pack is disconnected again; robot identity anchors remain unchanged.

**Frozen start keyframe:** one still instant with the connector visibly separated, train at rest, child's gaze held on the motor, and one orange gripper indicating the loose-contact area without pressure having begun. Compose the functional objects and both participants as one readable test setup. No movement, connection, press, release, or changed understanding is already present.

### Motion-spec intention

Start exactly from that disconnected frozen state. Once the accepted supervision condition is present, the pack is connected for the test. The robot redirects the child's attention from motor to contact. Using the approved safe participation method, robot and child jointly apply pressure to the loose contact; only after firm contact does the train move. They observe that result, release the same contact, and the train stops again. Let the child's gaze compare contact and train so the inference is visible without invented dialogue. After the stop has registered, disconnect the pack. Keep camera, set, train path, robot identity, and non-target parts stable. The end report must separately confirm press released, train stopped, pack disconnected, child having received the comparison, and all robot anchors intact. Duration, dialogue/narration, sound, supervisor identity, and child-contact choreography remain unresolved; if an accepted duration cannot preserve the connect–press–move–release–stop–disconnect chain and reaction, the storyboard owner must split the coverage rather than accelerate or omit a causal step.

### Reviewer checklist

**Evidence gates — revise if violated**

- Robot retains square head, one blue left antenna, and orange insulated grippers in start, motion, and end.
- Battery is disconnected outside the supervised test; the motion does not silently begin connected or finish connected.
- The same loose contact is pressed and released; movement begins under pressure and stops after release.
- The child's misconception changes because the contrasting results are visible, not because invented labels or dialogue declare the answer.
- The keyframe contains only the disconnected, unpressed start state.
- Hand/contact action is physically ordered and the exact end is reported rather than treated as a new authority.
- No unaccepted readable labels enter the image; any explanatory text remains a postproduction reservation.
- Final choreography has an accepted supervision arrangement and safe participation method.

**Taste choices — do not block without an accepted override**

- Exact 3D palette/material finish, camera height, reaction intensity, train speed, amount of anticipation, and postproduction graphic style.
- Whether the causal demonstration stays in one continuous shot or is later split for feasibility, provided every causal state and the child's receiving reaction remain covered once.

**Unresolved:** supervision arrangement; safe method for the child's joint press; child identity/Look; robot's non-anchor design; train/contact geometry; environment; duration; dialogue/narration/sound; postproduction text need, wording, and placement.
