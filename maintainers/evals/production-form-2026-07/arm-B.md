## Scene A — vertical ink-brush dynamic comic

### Director-facing note

Make the lamp an instrument of choice, not decoration. Hold the pawnshop geography in one legible vertical axis: counter between the pair, shelves behind the clerk, river window behind the collector. The start lets the clerk and audience see the seal in the lamp’s pool while her recognition stays controlled; the turn of the wick must read as deliberate before the story explains its motive. Ink density and negative space may intensify as the light contracts, but must not obscure the hand action, move the props, or replace the fixed geography.

### Essential asset / image-prompt decisions

- **Characters:** clerk and debt collector are separate identities. “Young” is the only accepted clerk descriptor; neither character has accepted face, body, hair, wardrobe, or color anchors. Character/Look prompts therefore remain provisional and must not invent them.
- **Location/View:** one riverside pawnshop identity; one blackout View for this scene. Fixed anchors are the counter, shelves behind the clerk, and river window behind the collector. Do not invent entrances, signs, weather, or additional light sources. A reusable location plate should be empty of cast and prove these relationships before adding atmosphere.
- **Oil lamp:** one tracked prop with an operable wick control. Start state: lit and held in the clerk’s left hand. End state: wick lowered, lamp still in that left hand. Exact lamp form, material, color, and flame treatment are unresolved.
- **Folded notice:** one tracked prop, continuously in the collector’s right hand. Its seal is a required graphic carrier; body copy is not accepted. For this shot, keep all copy unreadable/unshown and treat the seal as **graphic-only**. Do not invent characters, names, numbers, or heraldry. The seal’s exact design is unresolved and blocks a final reusable prop plate.
- **Prompt priority:** vertical ink-brush comic style; then accepted identity/state bindings; then counter/shelves/window geography; then lamp–seal visibility; then exclusions. Exclude extra lamps, a hand transfer, an unfolded notice, readable copy, or a moved window/shelf/counter. Do not use generic quality language in place of those facts.

### One shot + frozen keyframe intention

**Shot purpose:** the audience registers that the clerk deliberately hides the seal, while the reason for her recognition remains withheld.

- **Start boundary:** vertical medium two-shot across the counter. The clerk is on the shelves side, holding the lit lamp in her left hand; the collector is on the river-window side, holding the folded notice in his right. The seal sits inside the lamp’s visible pool. Her gaze has found it, but her face and posture do not disclose an explanation.
- **End boundary:** the clerk’s right hand has turned the wick down; her left still holds the lamp. The collector’s right still holds the folded notice. Neither prop has transferred or changed hands. The contracted light no longer reveals the seal; counter, shelves, window, positions, and axis remain coherent.
- **Camera intention:** one fixed, uninterrupted composition. Let the change in the lamp pool redirect attention; do not add a cut or ornamental move that could make the concealment feel accidental.
- **Frozen start keyframe:** the exact start boundary only—seal visible in the lamp pool, clerk’s right hand not yet on the wick, both required hands and props legible, fixed geography intact. It contains no dimming, hand travel, expression change, or camera movement.

### Motion-spec intention

- **Start anchor:** inherit the frozen frame exactly: lamp in clerk’s left hand; notice in collector’s right; seal visible; counter separates them.
- **Ordered motion:** the clerk holds her gaze on the seal for a restrained beat, suppresses any overt recognition, then brings her free right hand to the wick control and lowers it. The light pool contracts until the seal is no longer visible. The collector’s notice hand does not move or release.
- **Performance:** trigger = seeing the seal; processing = a controlled stillness rather than an explanatory reaction; choice = lowering the wick; landing = composed concealment. Do not add a flashback, dialogue, confession, or new clue.
- **Camera / sound / timing:** camera remains fixed. Use relative phases—brief recognition hold, deliberate adjustment, end hold—because no duration or audio has been accepted. Add no unsourced dialogue, music, or sound event.
- **End report:** must match the shot end boundary field-for-field, especially left-hand lamp, right-hand notice, seal hidden by reduced light rather than by prop movement, and unchanged geography.

## Scene B — stylized 3D educational short

### Director-facing note

Teach through an observable, reversible test rather than a lecture. Keep the loose contact and the train in the same causal view: pressure makes the train move; release makes it stop. The child’s attention must migrate from the presumed motor fault to the contact because of that repeatable result. Preserve the robot’s identity anchors in every view. Treat connection as a supervised test window only: the battery pack is disconnected before the test and disconnected again when the test is over.

### Essential asset / image-prompt decisions

- **Robot identity:** square head, a single blue antenna on the robot’s left, orange insulated grippers. These are stable anchors, not temporary state. Exclude a second/right antenna, mirrored antenna placement, non-blue antenna, or non-orange/uninsulated-looking grippers. Other robot proportions, materials, facial display, and colors are unresolved; do not invent identity-critical details.
- **Child:** a separate identity, but no accepted face, body, hair, wardrobe, age band, gender presentation, or palette is supplied. Keep the child prompt provisional and neutral rather than filling those gaps.
- **Location/View:** no setting geography has been accepted. Do not silently turn it into a classroom, workshop, home, or branded lab. The production view only needs a clear supervised test surface and sightlines once the location is chosen.
- **Props/states:** track the toy train, battery pack, and loose contact separately. Required chain: train stopped + pack disconnected + contact released → supervised connection → contact jointly pressed + train moving → contact released + train stopped → pack disconnected. Exact train, pack, connector, and contact geometry/materials are unresolved.
- **Prompt purpose:** a stylized-3D robot identity sheet can proceed only with the three accepted anchors; an apparatus prop plate and final child/scene prompts remain provisional until the missing designs/geography are accepted. Keep generated surfaces free of explanatory copy. If teaching text is required, reserve a clean overlay-safe area and add exact wording only in postproduction; do not invent labels, diagrams, brand marks, or UI text.

### One shot + frozen keyframe intention

**Shot purpose:** one uninterrupted cause-and-effect demonstration changes the child’s diagnosis from “motor” to “loose contact.”

- **Start boundary:** battery pack physically disconnected; contact loose and untouched; toy train stopped. Robot and child are positioned to observe both contact and train. The robot’s square head, single blue left antenna, and orange insulated grippers are visible. The child’s attention still favors the train/motor area; no label states the hypothesis.
- **End boundary:** after the supervised test, the contact is released, the train is stopped again, and the battery pack is disconnected. Robot and child remain with the apparatus; the child’s attention now rests on the contact as the changed diagnosis’s visible consequence. No prop has teleported or changed design.
- **Camera intention:** one locked, uninterrupted view that keeps the joint press and train response simultaneously legible; no cut may manufacture the causal link.
- **Frozen start keyframe:** the exact start boundary only—separated battery connection, released contact, stopped train, robot and child poised but not pressing, accepted robot anchors visible, and clean space reserved for optional postproduction text. It contains no connection, pressing, train motion, release, or belief-change arc.

### Motion-spec intention

- **Start anchor:** inherit the keyframe exactly, including the disconnected pack and untouched contact.
- **Ordered motion:** under the robot’s supervision, establish the temporary battery connection; robot and child jointly apply pressure to the loose contact; hold long enough for both to observe the train move; both release the contact; hold long enough to observe the train stop; end the supervised test by disconnecting the pack.
- **Performance:** trigger = the train moving only under pressure; processing = the child checks the moving train and the pressed contact; confirmation = release produces a stop; landing = the child looks to the contact rather than back to the motor. Keep the robot’s instruction readable through demonstration; add no unaccepted dialogue or labels.
- **Camera / sound / timing:** camera remains locked so both cause and effect stay in one evidentiary view. Use relative phases, not fixed seconds. Any apparatus/train sound must be separately accepted; do not add music, voiceover, or lip-synced explanation by assumption.
- **Preserve set:** robot identity anchors; child identity/Look once accepted; apparatus geometry; number and ownership of props; postproduction-only text policy; location anchors once accepted. Only pack connection, contact pressure, train movement, gaze, and supervised hand/gripper positions may change.
- **End report:** must equal the authored end: contact released, train stopped, pack disconnected, robot and child present, child attention redirected to the contact. Motion may not redefine the next shot or scene.
